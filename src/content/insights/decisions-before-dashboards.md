## The question

What does a reader need to understand or decide after looking at an analytical tool?

## Proposed argument

The question should determine the measures and comparisons. More charts do not automatically make the decision clearer.

## Planned structure

1. Identify the reader, decision and relevant time horizon.
2. Choose the baseline and comparison that explain the change.
3. Separate observed data, estimates and assumptions.
4. Make uncertainty and data limitations visible near the result.
5. Check whether the reader can explain what the output supports.

## Public reference to examine

[Automated Psychometrics](/work/automated-psychometrics/) provides a public example of statistical software accompanied by a methods paper and interpretable output. The future article would examine that design, without inferring adoption or business impact from availability alone.

## Example and evidence to develop

Create a synthetic scenario comparison and a short reader task. Document what each view helps the reader assess. Review the [published autopsych paper](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0257682) and distinguish its reported findings from any new interpretation.

## Intended takeaway

A decision tool should help the reader assess an action and its assumptions.
