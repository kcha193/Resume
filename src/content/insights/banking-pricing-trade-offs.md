## The question

How can a pricing recommendation make both its commercial rationale and its limitations easy to assess?

## Proposed argument

A useful recommendation starts with the decision, defines the objective and shows what changes under different assumptions. A model output is one input to that decision.

## Planned structure

1. Define the decision, time horizon and customer outcome to assess.
2. Show a transparent baseline before adding response modelling.
3. Compare scenarios across volume, margin and customer outcomes.
4. Identify uncertain assumptions and test how they change the recommendation.
5. Set out the evidence, review and monitoring needed before action.

## Example to develop

Use a wholly synthetic pricing scenario with invented inputs clearly marked as illustrative. Compare a baseline with alternative customer-response assumptions. Do not use employer data, models or pricing decisions.

## Evidence to gather

- Public methodological sources on response estimation and sensitivity analysis.
- Applicable published guidance on customer outcomes and model risk, checked before writing.
- Reproducible calculations for the synthetic example, including boundary cases.

## Intended takeaway

Make it clear what would change the recommendation, as well as what supports it.
